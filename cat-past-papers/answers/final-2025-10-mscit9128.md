## Question 1 (40 marks): Big Data precision farming

> Note: parts 2, 4, 5 and 7 say "city" although the scenario is farming; swap sensors for traffic cameras and the same answers apply.

### 1.1 Identify the 5 V's of Big Data in this project (5 marks)

- **Volume:** thousands of soil sensors plus drone and satellite images, terabytes per season.
- **Velocity:** sensor readings every few seconds; pest alerts needed the same day.
- **Variety:** sensor values, images, weather feeds, farm records.
- **Veracity:** faulty sensors, cloud cover on satellite images.
- **Value:** better yield forecasts, less water and fertilizer wasted, early outbreak warnings.

### 1.2 Describe how data sources, storage, processing and analysis fit into one architecture (6 marks)

- **Sources:** soil sensors, weather stations, drones, satellites, farm records.
- **Ingestion:** Kafka for sensor streams; bulk upload for images.
- **Storage:** HDFS or S3 data lake, Parquet by date and farm.
- **Processing:** Spark Streaming for live alerts; Spark batch for nightly cleaning and features.
- **Analysis:** Spark MLlib for yield and disease models; rules turn predictions into plans.
- **Serving:** dashboards for policymakers, SMS alerts for farmers.

### 1.3 Compare the suitability of Hadoop and Spark for this project (6 marks)

| Point | Hadoop (HDFS + MapReduce) | Spark |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Batch only, writes to disk each step | In memory, batch and streaming |
| Speed | Slow for iterative work | Much faster for iterative jobs |
| Real-time | Not suitable | Structured Streaming with Kafka |
| Machine learning | No built-in library | MLlib |
| Storage | HDFS stores data cheaply | Stores nothing; reads HDFS or S3 |

Verdict: use both. HDFS stores, Spark processes.

### 1.4 Illustrate how Kafka could manage the real-time data streams (5 marks)

- **Producers:** sensor gateways, weather service, drone upload service.
- **Topics per data type:** `soil-moisture`, `weather`, `pest-traps`, `drone-image-events`.
- **Partitions by `farm_id`:** order kept per farm, farms processed in parallel.
- **Consumer groups:** alerting and archiving read the same topics independently.
- **Replication factor 3, 7-day retention:** no data loss on one broker failure; replay after a failed job.

```
Sensor gateways --> soil-moisture (by farm_id) --> Spark Streaming --> irrigation alert
Weather API     --> weather                    --> archiver --> HDFS / S3
```

### 1.5 Analyze the role of predictive and prescriptive analytics (6 marks)

- **Predictive (what will happen):** forecast yield per field from soil, weather and images.
- **Predictive:** warn of pest or disease outbreaks days ahead from humidity and trap counts.
- **Prescriptive (what to do):** irrigation timing and amount from predicted moisture and rain.
- **Prescriptive:** fertilizer dose per zone, less waste and runoff.
- **Prescriptive:** rank farms for inspection when an outbreak is likely.

### 1.6 Evaluate the risks to data veracity and recommend ways to improve data quality (6 marks)

- **Risks:** faulty sensors, missing readings, cloudy images, timestamp errors.
- **Validation at ingestion:** range checks (moisture 0 to 100%) and schema checks.
- **Anomaly detection:** z-scores or comparison with neighbouring sensors.
- **Imputation:** interpolate short gaps and mark them as filled.
- **Cloud masking:** combine several satellite passes.
- **Calibration:** regular sensor checks and an owner per source.

### 1.7 Propose one new application of spatial analytics in this project (6 marks)

Variable-rate management zones:
- **Combine** GPS soil readings, drone NDVI and yield history on a GIS map.
- **Cluster** each field into zones with similar needs (k-means on location and soil).
- **Hotspot analysis** (Getis-Ord Gi*) finds pest clusters.
- **Act:** send zone plans to GPS-guided tractors and drip systems.
- **Benefit:** inputs only where needed, outbreaks contained early.
- **Policy:** regional risk maps guide subsidies.

Remember: 5 V's, HDFS stores Spark processes, topics partitions consumer groups.

## Question 2 (20 marks): Fraud detection at a local bank

### 2.1 Explain why stream processing might be preferred over batch processing (4 marks)

- **Timing:** fraud must be stopped before money leaves; nightly batch finds it too late.
- **Per transaction:** each one is scored within milliseconds to seconds.
- **Action:** block the card or ask for an OTP while the customer is at the till.
- **Patterns:** bursts of small test payments appear in short windows.

### 2.2 Classify the task as classification or regression and justify (4 marks)

- **Binary classification:** output is fraud or not fraud.
- **Supervised:** labels come from confirmed fraud cases and chargebacks.
- **Probability plus threshold:** a score such as 0.87 is compared with a cut-off.
- **Not regression:** no continuous number is predicted.

### 2.3 Identify two evaluation metrics suitable for imbalanced data (4 marks)

- **Accuracy fails:** with 0.1% fraud, "never fraud" scores 99.9% and catches nothing.
- **Recall** = TP / (TP + FN): share of real frauds caught.
- **Precision** = TP / (TP + FP): share of alerts that are real fraud.
- **Also:** F1, PR-AUC.

### 2.4 Discuss how real-time alerts could reduce losses (4 marks)

- **Block or hold** the payment before it completes.
- **Freeze the card** after the first suspicious use.
- **Confirm by SMS** so stolen cards are reported in minutes.
- **Lower costs:** fewer chargebacks, more trust.

### 2.5 Suggest one strategy to reduce false alarms (4 marks)

Tune the decision threshold using business costs:
- **Pick the threshold** where the cost of false alarms plus missed fraud is lowest, not a fixed 0.5.
- **Two levels:** high score blocks, medium score asks the customer to confirm.
- **Feedback loop:** analyst decisions become new labels; retrain regularly.

Remember: binary classification, recall and precision, threshold tuning.

## Question 3 (20 marks): Predicting student dropout on an online learning platform

### 3.1 Describe how predictive analytics could identify at-risk students (4 marks)

- **Features per week:** logins, video completion, quiz scores, late assignments, days since last login.
- **Train** a classifier (logistic regression, random forest) on past dropped and completed students.
- **Score weekly:** high dropout probability flags the student.
- **Explain the flag** (e.g. no login for 10 days) so tutors can help.

### 3.2 Compare descriptive and predictive analytics in this context (4 marks)

| | Descriptive | Predictive |
|---|---|---|
| Question | What happened? | What is likely to happen? |
| Example | 18% dropped out last term | This student has a 75% dropout risk |
| Methods | Counts, averages, dashboards | ML models on past outcomes |

### 3.3 Suggest one data visualization tool for tracking learning progress (4 marks)

**Power BI** (or Tableau):
- **Line charts** of weekly logins and quiz scores.
- **Heat map** of video completion shows where students give up.
- **Filters and drill-down** from class to student, with alerts when a metric drops.

### 3.4 Explain how ethical considerations apply when analyzing student data (4 marks)

- **Privacy and consent:** collect only what is needed, with consent.
- **Security:** limited access, encryption, access logs.
- **Fair use:** a risk score triggers support, never punishment.
- **Bias:** weak internet lowers logins, not commitment.

### 3.5 Propose ways to balance model accuracy and fairness (4 marks)

- **Remove sensitive attributes** and check for proxies such as device type.
- **Measure per group:** equal opportunity (recall, false positive rate) and demographic parity.
- **Fix gaps** by reweighting data or per-group thresholds, accepting a small accuracy loss.
- **Human in the loop:** tutors review flags; audit every term.

Remember: logins and quiz features, what happened vs what will happen, support not punishment.

## Question 4 (20 marks): Machine learning on X-ray images in a hospital

### 4.1 Explain how deep learning can assist in image recognition tasks (4 marks)

- **CNNs** learn features directly from pixels.
- **Layers:** early ones detect edges, deeper ones detect nodules, opacities, fractures.
- **Transfer learning:** fine-tune a pre-trained network (ResNet, DenseNet) on fewer labelled X-rays.
- **Output:** probability per condition, optionally with age and symptoms.

### 4.2 Describe how data preprocessing improves image quality before modeling (4 marks)

- **Resize and crop** to a fixed size (e.g. 224 x 224); remove borders and text.
- **Normalize** pixel values so images from different machines are comparable.
- **Denoise and enhance contrast** (Gaussian filter, histogram equalization) to show faint lesions.
- **Augment** with small rotations, flips and brightness changes to reduce overfitting.

### 4.3 Identify challenges of model interpretability in healthcare (4 marks)

- **Black box:** doctors cannot see why the model says "disease".
- **Accountability:** doctors and regulators need checkable reasons.
- **Wrong signals:** the model may use a scanner artefact or hospital marker.
- **Tools are approximate:** Grad-CAM and SHAP need clinical checking.

### 4.4 Discuss how bias might appear in medical datasets (4 marks)

- **Sampling bias:** one hospital, scanner or population.
- **Under-representation:** few children, older patients or rare conditions.
- **Label bias:** radiologists disagree, or access to tests is unequal.
- **Historical bias:** groups diagnosed late in the past are labelled "healthy".

### 4.5 Suggest techniques to ensure fairness and transparency (4 marks)

- **Diverse data** from several hospitals and scanners.
- **Per-group metrics:** sensitivity and specificity by age, sex and site.
- **Explanations:** Grad-CAM heat map with each prediction; a model card stating limits.
- **Doctor decides:** log predictions for audit and monitor after deployment.

Remember: CNN and transfer learning, resize normalize augment, per-group metrics.

## Question 5 (20 marks): Inventory and demand forecasting for a retail company

### 5.1 Explain how temporal analytics could improve demand forecasting (4 marks)

- **Trend and seasonality:** growth over months; weekends, paydays, holidays.
- **Lag features:** last week's sales, same week last year, weather, promotions.
- **Models:** ARIMA, Prophet, or gradient boosting with date features.
- **Result:** fewer stockouts before holidays, less overstock after.

### 5.2 Describe how spatial analytics can help identify regional demand patterns (4 marks)

- **Map sales** by branch and region on a GIS map.
- **Find hotspots**, e.g. cold drinks in warmer regions.
- **Link to local factors:** weather, population, income, competitors.
- **Act:** stock branches differently, pick new sites.

### 5.3 Differentiate between operational and descriptive analytics in this case (4 marks)

| | Descriptive | Operational |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Summarize the past | Run daily actions |
| Example | Rice sales rose 15% in the North | Stock below reorder point, reorder sent |
| Timing | Weekly, monthly | Near real time |

### 5.4 Propose how real-time dashboards could support decision-making (4 marks)

- **One screen:** live sales, stock and forecast per branch.
- **Alerts:** low stock, sales spikes, weak promotions.
- **Same-day action:** move stock or change prices.

### 5.5 Evaluate the limitations of relying only on analytics for strategic decisions (4 marks)

- **Past-based:** models fail after sudden change (pandemic, new competitor).
- **Data quality:** wrong stock counts give confident wrong answers.
- **Missing context:** brand, suppliers, staff knowledge.
- **Correlation is not causation:** combine analytics with human judgement.

Remember: trend seasonality lags, GIS hotspots, analytics plus judgement.

## Question 6 (20 marks): Sentiment analysis and social media data

> Note: the scenario is bank fraud but all five parts ask about social media sentiment, so the answers follow the parts as asked.

### 6.1 Explain how sentiment analysis extracts opinions from social media data (4 marks)

- **Collect** posts by API, keyword or hashtag (e.g. mentions of the bank).
- **Clean** text: remove links, handle emojis and slang, tokenize.
- **Classify** as positive, negative or neutral (lexicon scores or ML models such as BERT).
- **Aggregate** by topic, time and place.

### 6.2 Discuss the benefits of combining text analytics with visualization tools (4 marks)

- **Fast reading:** trend lines, word clouds, topic charts.
- **Spikes stand out**, e.g. negative sentiment on the day the app fails.
- **Maps and filters** show where and who.
- **Shared dashboards** for all teams.

### 6.3 Suggest how real-time analytics can enhance marketing responses (4 marks)

- **Catch complaint waves** within minutes, before they spread.
- **Measure campaigns live** and adjust the same day.
- **Route complaints** to customer care for quick replies.

### 6.4 Assess the challenges posed by fake accounts and misinformation (4 marks)

- **Bots** distort sentiment up or down.
- **False stories** spread faster than corrections and damage trust.
- **Sarcasm and fake reviews** lower model accuracy.

### 6.5 Recommend measures to improve data reliability (4 marks)

- **Bot detection:** account age, posting rate, follower ratio, duplicate text.
- **Credibility weighting** and removal of duplicates and spam.
- **Fact-check** viral claims against trusted sources.
- **Validate** on hand-labelled samples; add survey data.

Remember: collect clean classify aggregate, act faster, bot detection.
